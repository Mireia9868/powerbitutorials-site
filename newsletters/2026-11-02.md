---
subject: "The fiscal year trap in TOTALYTD"
preheader: "Why your YTD is six months off and nothing errors"
---

If you have ever shipped a year-to-date number that looked fine in July and
was quietly wrong by six months, this is probably why.

`TOTALYTD` assumes your fiscal year ends on 31 December:

```dax
Sales YTD = TOTALYTD ( [Total Sales], 'Date'[Date] )
```

It compiles. It returns a number. It is wrong for any company whose fiscal year
doesn't start in January — and it will not warn you, because as far as DAX is
concerned nothing unusual happened. You asked for "year to date" and it gave you
"calendar year to date".

The fix is the third argument, which is the fiscal year *end* as `"MM-DD"`:

```dax
-- Fiscal year ends 30 June
Sales YTD = TOTALYTD ( [Total Sales], 'Date'[Date], "06-30" )
```

Two things worth knowing beyond the syntax:

**It's not only YTD.** `TOTALQTD` takes the same argument, and a wrong fiscal
year quietly breaks quarter-to-date, variance-to-date, and anything built on top
of them. One missing string can misstate a whole report page.

**The check takes 30 seconds.** Open a month in the first half of your fiscal
year. If YTD for that month starts in January instead of your fiscal year start,
the argument is missing or wrong. Do this before anyone sees the report, not
after.

This is bug #1 in the pack you downloaded, and it's the one I've shipped most
often. If you want to check whether your model has it right now: put Month on
rows, add `Sales YTD`, and pick a month before your fiscal year ends.
